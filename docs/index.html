Measuring the shared heat risk of America's AI data centers

November 2026 · Valmik Nahata, Varun Sinha, Rishab Kolan, Rishil Chintalapati · UC San Diego

We present a fleet-level analysis of heat risk for America's planned AI data centers. Combining ten CMIP6 climate models with Epoch AI's map of 77 planned campuses, we find that one heat wave can reach a large share of the fleet at once, that such shared heat days become common as the planet warms, and that where new capacity is built can reduce the risk.

AI data centers are approved one at a time: through federal permitting7, on federal land8 and in grid connection rules9,10. Climate risk studies also assess sites one at a time15,16. Heat waves, however, span hundreds of kilometers and can stress several power grids at once17. Here we describe how this shared exposure grows as the planet warms.

A fleet built in a few years

Planned AI capacity in the United States grows from 0.1 GW in early 2023 to 43 GW by 2030, according to construction timelines compiled by Epoch AI1. Most of it sits in a few clusters across the Midwest, Texas and the Southeast.

Conclusion

Heat risk to AI infrastructure is shared: one heat wave reaches many campuses and several power grids at once, and these events become more frequent and longer as the planet warms. We suggest that permitting, federal site selection and interconnection studies assess the fleet jointly7,8,9, that curtailment and backup rules plan for multi-day, multi-region events6,12, and that new capacity favor places whose heat rarely coincides with the rest of the fleet.

Data and method

Daily maximum temperature from ten CMIP6 models2 (; first member each) via the public cloud archive3, for 1995–2014 and 2041–2060 and 2081–2100 under SSP2-4.5 (moderate) and SSP5-8.5 (high). Extreme heat is relative to each model's own 1995–2014 99th percentile at each grid cell, so model bias cancels. A shared heat day has at least a quarter of planned power in extreme heat. Grid regions are approximated by states. Sites are Epoch AI's frontier data centers1, geocoded and weighted by planned power. Results hold for 98th to 99.5th percentile thresholds and 25% to 50% fleet shares. Heat stands in for grid stress; it is not a record of emergencies. Code and data: repository link.
