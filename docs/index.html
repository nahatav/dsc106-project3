Choosing where to build AI data centers as heat waves grow

November 2026 ·
Valmik Nahata, Varun Sinha, Rishab Kolan and Rishil Chintalapati, UC San Diego

AI data centers are approved one site at a time, but a heat wave can reach many of them at once, on the same days that households lean hardest on the grid. Using ten CMIP6 climate models and Epoch AI's records of 77 planned US campuses, we score every county in the lower 48 as a place to build and show where the next gigawatt would add the least shared risk to the fleet and to the people who rely on the same power.

Siting for shared heat is not only an engineering choice. When AI load clusters where heat strikes together, the cost of a bad summer falls on everyone connected to those grids, through emergency curtailment, higher prices or outages18,19. Spreading new capacity toward places that are rarely hot when the rest of the fleet is protects those communities as well as the companies.

Data and method

Daily maximum temperature from ten CMIP6 models2 (; first member each) via the public cloud archive3, for 1995–2014 and for 2041–2060 and 2081–2100 under SSP2-4.5 (moderate) and SSP5-8.5 (high emissions). A place is in extreme heat on a day above its own 1995–2014 99th percentile in that model (its design day), so model biases cancel. A shared heat day has at least a quarter of planned US AI power in extreme heat. Sites are Epoch AI's frontier data centers1, geocoded and weighted by planned power. Global warming levels add 0.85°C to each model's warming since 1995–201423. Siting criteria are computed on a common 1° grid, take the ten-model median and are sampled at county centroids, so county values inherit the roughly 100 km resolution of the climate models. Each criterion is ranked across about 3,100 counties and the ranks are averaged; the ten best areas are chosen greedily at least 500 km apart. Grid regions and their regulators are approximated by states, and DOE site coordinates beyond the four selected sites are approximate. Figure 6 codes each measure from the cited legal and news summaries; it reflects what each measure addresses as described there, not a full legal analysis. Results hold for 98th to 99.5th percentile thresholds and 25% to 50% fleet shares. Heat stands in for grid stress; it is not a record of emergencies.
